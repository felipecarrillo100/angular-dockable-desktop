# M9 — Sidebar, secondary sidebar, toolbar · **PASS**

**Gate:** `npm run gate -- M9` (standing gate, M9 rules, browser gate). 599/599 unit tests.

## What exists

- **`<ndd-sidebar>`**: the activity bar plus a resizable drawer.
  - rdd's eight handle methods are four **`model()`s**, each bindable as `[( )]`:
    `activeTabId`, `visible`, `stripVisible` and `width`.
  - Tab content comes from an `<ng-template nddSidebarTab="id">` (which wins) or the tab's
    `component` + `inputs`. `<ng-template nddSidebarHeader>` replaces the drawer header. Both
    templates are **typed** through `ngTemplateContextGuard`: `let-tab`, `close` and `open`.
  - It keeps vdd's behaviour:
    - lazy mounting, `eagerMount` and `preserveState`;
    - hidden tabs that stay openable;
    - header and footer rail areas mixing tabs, action buttons and caller-rendered entries;
    - auto-close when the open tab disappears (never falling back to another tab);
    - resize clamped at the drag, with the render bounded by min/max;
    - a dev warning when `showCloseButton` can have no effect.
- **`<ndd-secondary-sidebar>`**: the same base class and **the same template string** as the
  primary. It takes the opposite side from the primary it finds via `skipSelf`, and refuses to
  exist without a primary or nested inside another secondary.
- **`injectSidebar()`** resolves to the nearest sidebar. Its `activeTabId` is a signal, and
  `position` is read live, since inputs aren't set when the context is built.
  **`injectSidebarTab()`** resolves per pane: each pane gets its own `Injector`, used for the
  tab template, the header template and the tab component alike.
- **`<ndd-toolbar>`**: action, radio, toggle, group and separator items as data, with rdd's
  field names.
  - `[(visible)]` collapses it.
  - "Controlled" means the field is *present*, including `false` or `null`.
  - A group's flyout is portalled to `document.body`. It is dismissed by a capture-phase
    pointerdown outside, by Escape, or by the toolbar being destroyed.
- **`injectToolbar()`** returns the workspace's toolbar state, with no provider.

**Driving code.**
- vdd's `setProps` is `setInput`, a one-way `[x]`.
- vdd's `onUpdate:x` listener is the model's own output (`model.subscribe`).
- The twins use **`twoWayBinding`**, which is what `[(x)]` compiles to. A component created
  with bindings accepts no `setInput`, so its other inputs go through `inputBinding`.
- Two further hosts use real `[( )]` template syntax, on a primary and on a secondary.
- The click helper is `HTMLElement.click()`, which, like a real click and like vdd's `trigger`,
  does nothing on a disabled button. `dispatchEvent` fires listeners regardless in jsdom.

## M9 rules (`scripts/gates/m9.mjs`)

After vdd's M9 gate, adapted:
- **D12:** the structural rules are in CSS, and only named per-render values are bound inline.
  Binding a whole `[style]` object is rejected.
- One template and one base class shared by both sidebars; the rail and drawer each rendered
  exactly once.
- Per-pane injectors, and `descendants: false` queries, so a primary never picks up its
  secondary's templates.
- A per-instance resize flag (SB47).
- Toolbar state on the workspace, with no provider.
- Controlled-means-present checks.
- Flyout portal and every listener-removal path.
- Collapsed-size-only inline styles.
- **D13:** prefixed keyframes and `animation:` names.
- Explicit rail discriminators.

The Angular addition is **the twin rule**. It reads vdd's own `sidebar.test.ts` and
`toolbar.test.ts`, and derives every test that writes a model (`setProps({ visible… })`) or
listens to one (`onUpdate:`). That gives SB10, 13, 18, 21, 22, 24, 27, 28, 30, 37, 42, 50, 51
and TB20. Each must have a *passing* test whose title starts `[(` and names that number. The
first derivation found five untwinned (SB28/SB30, SB37, SB42, TB20); twins were written for
them, so none were dropped.

## Browser gate (real Chrome, zoneless **and** zone.js)

The playground's `?chrome` mode nests primary and secondary sidebars around a toolbar and the
desktop, all bound through real `[( )]`.

- **Layout:** the primary sits on the left edge and the secondary on the right; the strip fills
  the column.
- **Open and close:** a real click writes `[(activeTabId)]`, and the drawer renders at the bound
  width with its template content.
- **Resize:** a real 60 px drag gives width 340 and the drawer renders 340; a drag past
  `maxWidth` gives exactly 480.
- **D12:** with a 3000 px-wide element inside the workspace, the drawer still renders exactly
  its 300 px.
- **Survival:** the workspace panel is the same node, mounted once and never unmounted, through
  open, close, resize and hiding the whole sidebar.
- **N6:** on an RTL page the left sidebar renders at the right edge, and a 40 px drag away from
  the edge grows it by 40.
- **Flyout:** checked under LTR, a mirrored RTL app, and an RTL workspace in an unmirrored page
  (N5):
  - it is outside the strip, inside the viewport, and beside the strip on the correct side;
  - `elementFromPoint` at an item's centre is that item;
  - a real click selects the tool, and the group button wears its icon.
- **Collapse:** `[(visible)]` false leaves the toolbar exactly 0 px wide (N7).

**Negative control** (`m9.mjs --control`, run by the M9 rules): restore rdd's
`flex-basis: auto` on the content wrapper. It is rejected with "D12: with a 3000px-wide panel
the drawer renders 180px, not its 300px".

## Findings

1. **N5: vdd mirrors the flyout on the workspace's direction, not the strip's.** This is right
   only when the app puts `dir` on the chrome too (vdd's demo does). The first browser run, with
   only the desktop RTL, showed the flyout opening leftwards off-screen and being clamped back
   over the strip. It now mirrors on the strip's computed direction.
2. **N6: vdd's sidebar resize ignores RTL.** In a right-to-left container the flex row
   reverses, so `position="left"` renders on the right edge, and its resizer grew it the wrong
   way. The drag now follows the physical side.
3. **N7: a collapsed toolbar kept its 1 px border.** Found by the browser gate's collapse check.
4. **The flyout clamp ran in jsdom against zero-size rects** and pulled every flyout to (8, 8).
   A flyout with no size has nothing to clamp, so the pass now skips it.
5. **Angular warned NG0956 on every tab switch** (the M4 leaf group's `track id` over its
   one-item selected list). Re-creating that slot is deliberate: it is how a tab switch hands
   the panel a new place. The loop now tracks a derived key, with a comment saying why.
6. **Lint:** `const self = this` for the context's live getters became `Object.defineProperties`.
